## London Shopping Areas: A Guide to the City's Shopping Streets

London does not have a shopping district. It has about forty of them, and the difference
between a good afternoon and a wasted one is knowing which street matches what you came for.

Oxford Street has the volume. Bond Street has the money. Regent Street has the flagships.
Those three are well documented and easy to find, and they are not what this guide is about.

The streets below are the ones where London still shops for itself. Each has a distinct
character, most of them earned over a century or more, and each rewards a different kind of
visit.

### Carnaby Street and Soho

Menswear built this neighbourhood in the 1960s and the bones never changed. The main
pedestrianised run is the famous part and the least interesting one. The shopping worth the
trip sits in Newburgh Quarter, a pocket of small independent shops immediately east, where
the premises are too small for chains to bother with.

Best for: considered menswear, contemporary womenswear, denim, footwear, and eating in
Kingly Court afterwards.

[Read the Carnaby Street guide](/shopping/carnaby-street)

### Kings Road, Chelsea

Two fashion revolutions started on this road. Mary Quant opened Bazaar here in 1955 and
put London on the map for the miniskirt. Twenty years later, punk was assembled at number
430. Today the eastern end near Sloane Square is the polished, high-street half, and it
gets more independent the further west you walk.

Best for: a long linear walk, mainstream fashion at the Sloane Square end, and Duke of
York Square for a break.

[Read the Kings Road guide](/shopping/kings-road)

### Notting Hill

Portobello Road is the reason to come, and Saturday is the only day the whole market runs.
Antiques at the southern end, vintage clothing under the Westway, and the further north you
push toward Golborne Road the less the neighbourhood is performing for visitors.

Best for: antiques, vintage, and the boutiques on Westbourne Grove and Ledbury Road if you
want the shopping without the crowds.

[Read the Notting Hill guide](/shopping/notting-hill)

### Kensington High Street

This street was once the most glamorous shopping in London. Biba occupied a seven-storey
Art Deco department store here, with a roof garden of a garden and a half acres, flamingos
included. That era ended in the seventies and the street has been more ordinary since.

Best for: efficient mainstream shopping in one straight line, and the best architecture of
any street on this list.

[Read the Kensington High Street guide](/shopping/kensington-high-street)

### Marylebone High Street

The quietest street here and, for many people, the best. In the mid-nineties a third of the
shops were empty. The estate that owns the street rebuilt it deliberately around
independents and specialists rather than chains, and it worked.

Best for: bookshops, food, homeware, and a Sunday farmers' market. The most pleasant place
in central London to spend two unhurried hours.

[Read the Marylebone High Street guide](/shopping/marylebone-high-street)

### How to choose

If you want one street and one afternoon, go to Marylebone.

If you want variety within a short walk, go to Carnaby.

If you want to cover distance and see how London changes as you walk it, go to Kings Road.

If it is Saturday, go to Portobello. If it is not Saturday, do not go to Portobello.

If you want to shop and be finished quickly, go to Kensington High Street.

### Go when you can

Every street here is worth the journey. London shopping is a walking activity and no
website replaces turning a corner into a shop you did not know was there.
